This extension turns the animator into a small spirograph loom, not a single-color line pen. Line still works. Three new forms sit beside it, and a fresh library includes a loom drawing whose pen distance is the shape slider.

Point 1 is the loom curve. Spiro(center, fixed, rolling, pen, turns) traces a roulette around center. fixed is the radius of the circle that stays put. rolling is the radius of the circle that moves: positive rolling travels inside the fixed circle, and negative rolling travels outside it, so one constructor covers both a hypotrochoid and an epitrochoid. pen is how far the nib sits from the moving circle's center, and turns is how many times that circle goes around. Passing shape_slider as pen makes the slider morph the rosette while the clock stays where it is, so a finished loom changes shape in place instead of replaying from empty. The library drawing is a medallion: a hoop, three threads crossing at the center, and eight small looms around the rim, each pen tied to the slider. Three smaller drawings, named spiro, ink, and stitch, show each piece by itself, and the code of each one says what the numbers mean. A short note under the drawing menu explains the same three pieces, and the sentence changes when spiro, ink, stitch, or loom is the open drawing. A rolling radius of zero is the degenerate loom, a circle of radius pen, rather than a separate circle tool that every other project would add.

Point 2 is two-stop ink. Ink(shape, r0, g0, b0, r1, g1, b1) wraps any shape, including a line or a loom. The six numbers are red, green, and blue from 0 to 255, a start ink and an end ink. The stroke is not painted in one flat color chosen by the app. The path's time window is split into short ribbons. Colour walks from the first triple to the second by the middle of the stroke, then back to the first, so a closed stitch meets itself in the same ink instead of showing a hard seam. Thickness pulses along that window, like yarn, rather than fading to a hairline at the join. A shape with no Ink keeps the app's single warm stroke, so the original examples look the same as before.

Point 3 is the closing stitch. Stitch(shape) appends the first sample again after the curve is built. A loom that would otherwise stop just short of its start becomes a closed rosette. Wrapped around a line, the pen travels out and then back home. Stitch and Ink compose in either order, so a program can seal a curve and still blend two colors along that closed route.

Point 5 is a second slider and a rose. Whirl(center, petals, reach, spin) draws a rhodonea, a flower of petals loops with radius reach. spin turns that whole flower, and 0 and 100 are one full rotation apart. The app's Spin slider is passed as the second argument of draw, and only when draw is written with two parameters, so every older drawing that takes just shape_slider keeps working and ignores the new slider. Dragging Spin redraws in place and does not restart the clock, so a finished rose turns instead of replaying. The whirl drawing uses Shape for the petal count and Spin for the turn, with a second smaller rose turning the other way.

Point 4 is how this stays inside the animator we already have. Spiro, Ink, and Stitch are constructors in the draw language, next to Point, Line, and Path, and the built-in reference lists them. The renderer samples the curve into the same RenderPath list the clock already reveals, so speed, pause, reverse, and the shape slider behave as they do for lines. Nothing here is a second canvas or a game pasted on top of the drawing.
